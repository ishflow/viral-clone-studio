# viraleditor accuracy sweep (Task C1, partial; round 2: Task C1b)

Question: is "what edit.json says in frames is what the mp4 shows in frames" true? Method: documents in `viraleditor/src/sweep/documents.ts` go through parseEdit, compose, writeComposition, `createRenderer` (check, render with defaults, quality looks), twice. Every frame of the mp4 is streamed and read (`analyse.ts`). Media are made so a pixel names what it shows: clips are one flat colour that encodes frame mod 64 (green and blue in steps of 24) plus the frame bar; pictures are flat; texts, cards and pills have red channel 255 and everything else has 0. The prediction per pixel comes from `timeline.json` (shots, opacity, move, zoom, fx layer, windows). Text, caption word and card presence and opacity are measured as ink mass against `timeline.animations`.

All commands ran: `VE_RENDER=1 timeout … pnpm vitest run src/sweep`. Machine load 28 to 40 during all renders (not asserted).

## Weak spot (a), blur against fade (closed)
The edge-sharpness idea fails: a blur keeps the total edge length of a stroke, so the measure read 1.03 to 1.08 for the blur exit and 1.03 to 1.15 for a fade (measured). What tells them apart is the share of ink that is as dark as a flat fill at the frame's opacity ("solid ink"): a fade keeps it at 0.98 to 1.00 on every exit frame; the blur exit falls to 0.96, 0.80, 0.60 on the last three frames (blur radius 8 px or more) because the radius ramps with ease `in`. Test: `controls.render.test.ts` (passes; also flags the same pixels when the table calls the blur a fade or a fade a blur). The first 4 to 5 frames of a 12-frame blur exit have a radius under 3 px and cannot be told from a fade by any measure.

## Controls (the net sees one frame)
`controls.render.test.ts`: a shot starting one source frame later, a cut one frame early and a text one frame late are all flagged on the same pixels.

## Limits found in review (2026-10-04, not yet fixed)
The sentence above is narrower than it reads. A review of the sweep's own code found:
- **The expectation is the engine's own timeline.** Each document's "table" is the timeline returned by the same `compose()` call that wrote the composition (`run.ts`). The sweep proves that the render shows what the timeline says, on the frame it says; it does not prove that the timeline says what the edit document asks for. An error shared by both passes. No assertion requires a document's texts, captions, cards or windows to exist, so an empty list passes with zero checks.
- **Only hard edges are exact to the frame.** For a text or a card that fades or pops over 6 frames or more, the thresholds of the judge (0.2 on, 0.12 off) let the enter or the exit be one frame early or late and still pass. Cuts, shots and hard-edged texts are exact, and the controls show that a one-frame shift of those is caught.
- **Captions are judged by ink, not by word.** The judge reads how much ink there is and whether the active mark moves; it does not read which word is lit.
- **Nothing is removed.** Every media folder and render folder of the sweep stays in the system temp folder.
These are carried to the continuation of this task, together with what "Not covered" lists.

### Finding 1, cause (Task C1c, 2026-10-05): CSS animations ran on the wall clock until HyperFrames first sought them

**Cause (composition side, made certain by runs).** Every animation of a composition is a CSS animation written inline (`animation: … backwards`, word marks `… 1 normal none`), running from the moment the page first draws. HyperFrames 0.8.120's runtime (CSS adapter, `packages/core/src/runtime/adapters/css.ts` in its own sources; the minified copy in `node_modules/hyperframes/dist/hyperframe-runtime.js`) seeks an animation by its handle (`currentTime = t − start`, `pause()`), and finds the handles only through `document.getAnimations()`. That call leaves out an animation that has already ended and fills nothing after its end (fill `none`, or `backwards` past its end). Such an element gets the adapter's fallback instead: inline `animation-play-state: paused` and `animation-delay: <delay − t>` (rounded to ms), which is right only if the animation still stands at 0; an ended one stands at its end, so it is frozen after its end on every later seek of that page. Whether this happens depends on the wall-clock time between the page's first drawn frame and the runtime's first seek, i.e. on load. The shortest such animation in the sweep is NOW's word mark (group 4's first word, one frame: it ends 16.7 ms after it starts), then the enters of the groups (150 to 330 ms).

**Evidence (all ran).**
- Probe with HyperFrames' own file server (runtime in the head, bridge in the body), its Chrome (headless shell 152.0.7977.30) and the engine's seek (`window.__hf.seek(59/30)`, then `(60/30)`), document 08: page as served, NOW lit 10 of 10 (all elements sought by handle, `currentTime` 0); with a body script answered 300 ms late (the page draws meanwhile), NOW unlit 10 of 10, and `cap-4` (enter, should be at opacity 0.49) at 1.00: both had no handle and carried the fallback delay `-0.017s` with no live animation.
- Real CLI renders (`hyperframes render`, quality looks, 4 workers) of document 08 with a 200 MB do-nothing script at the end of the body (the parser waits, the page draws): 3 of 3 renders leave NOW unlit (red, group at full opacity; 112 of 120 frames differ from a good render, from frame 8 on, since more short animations end before the runtime starts). The same with the fix below: 3 of 3 renders frame-identical (framemd5, 120 of 120) to the good render.
- Document 06 in the probe, the same 300 ms gap: frames 20 and 24 change (the `blur-in` enter is lost; PSNR 25.6 and 30.6 dB against the calm page), frames 46 to 56 do not.
- Natural rate in this round (no forced gap; machine load 6 to 50, partly from other agents, partly from up to 8 busy threads of mine): document 08, draft, 4 workers: 0 of 25 calm, 0 of 20 with 8 threads; looks, 8 workers, 8 threads: 0 of 30. The race did not occur on its own here; the sweep saw it at load up to 110 with other renders running. Workers 1 against 4 was not measured as a rate: the race is per page (each worker's page has its own), so the count only changes how many pages can lose it.
- Classification: neither capture nor encoding. The wrong frame is the page's state at the sought time; the small differences (up to 26 levels) on the other frames of the odd sweep renders are, assumed and not checked, the lost enter of group 4 (frames 60 to 64) and the encoder carrying the different frame 60 into its neighbours.

**Fix (ours).** `src/compose/css.ts` writes `#root, #root * { animation-play-state: paused !important; }`: an animation is created paused, stands at 0 until it is sought, and is then always found by `document.getAnimations()` and sought by handle (probe: every element `paused:0.0` at frame 60 with and without the gap). `MOTION_CSS` is unchanged. Test: `src/sweep/paused.render.test.ts` renders document 08 three times (calm; with the 200 MB gap; with the gap and the rule removed): the third must leave NOW unlit (the fault, reproduced on purpose), the second must light NOW and keep the red and green ink of the caption box within 3 % of the calm render on every frame (not framemd5, see the second face below). Ran: passed 4 times, 2 of them with 6 busy threads of mine (load up to 35). The third case is the state before the fix and is asserted to show the fault in every run; with the old css.ts the test stops in its setup, which checks that compose writes the rule (by reading, not run). `repeat.render.test.ts`: "every render lights NOW" now runs; "every render frame-identical" stays skipped (second face). Ran before that split, with the strict statement, 6 busy threads of mine and load about 30: 20 + 20 renders of documents 07 and 08, all frame-identical to their first, NOW lit in all (698 s, with the paused test). The sweep's handling of uneven renders (`uneven`, skipped identity) is left as it is.

**Second face, not this cause (open).** With the fix, 30 renders of document 08 with the 200 MB gap (draft, 4 workers, 8 busy threads, load 8 to 50): 1 differed from the others on frames 99 to 101 only, in one block of 24 x 16 px (x 456 to 479, y 784 to 799, the end of the G of the active word GO), by up to 112 levels on frame 99 and 24 to 29 on 100 and 101; seen enlarged, the same letters with slightly different edge pixels. A block-sized redraw difference of the page under load (rasterisation, or the capture), not a wrong time: assumed, not checked. It has the size and look of the 06 "cut O" below.

**Not explained (open).** Document 06, frames 46 to 49 (`blur-1` at rest, a corner of the "O" cut away): `blur-1`'s animations are exits with fill `forwards` that start at 1.45 s, so they are never left out; this face is not this cause. Thirty renders of the fixed document 06 (looks, 4 workers, load 18 to 32) were all frame-identical; the face was not seen again. Document 05's two frames (81, 82, up to 15 levels): not examined.

**Upstream (HyperFrames 0.8.120), for a bug report.** CSS adapter: an inline CSS animation with fill `none` (or `backwards`) that ends on the wall clock before the runtime's first seek is never returned by `document.getAnimations()`, so it is never sought by handle; the negative-delay fallback assumes the animation stands at 0, but it stands at its end, so the element stays in its after phase for the whole render. Minimal repro: a root composition at 30 fps with `<div class="clip" data-start="2" data-duration="1" data-track-index="1"><span style="animation: on 0.0333s step-end -0.0167s 1 normal none">X</span></div>`, `@keyframes on { from, to { color: lime } }`, and a parser-blocking `<script src="big.js"></script>` (a large comment-only file) at the end of the body: frame 60 shows X not lime; without the script, lime (this reduced form was not run; document 08 is the case that ran). Ways out upstream: hold every CSS animation paused from the start in render mode (a pre-head style), or in the fallback restart the animation (so it stands at 0) before setting the delay. The fallback's `toFixed(3)` on the delay also moves every edge by up to 0.5 ms.

### Promise against the edit (review finding 2): closed

`promise.ts` works out from edit.json alone (with the defaults the edit format documents, written again on purpose) what timeline.json must say: canvas and frame count; every shot's at, for, from, media, kind; every transition's kind, from, cut and length (a cut is a row of 0 frames); every window's for, behind, frames and swap pictures; every text line's at, for, rest (enter, exit and stagger) and words; every caption word's index, text and at, each group's at, end (next group, or the last word's end plus the hold), rest, word ends and lines; every card's at, for, media and rest; and that the timeline's texts, captions, cards and windows are not empty where the edit has them. `promise.test.ts` (fast, no render): all 14 documents agree; 14 falsified tables (a shot one frame late, a `from` +1, a transition one frame longer, a staggered line's rest one frame late, a text one frame shorter, a caption word one frame early, a group one frame longer, two caption words swapped, a card one frame late, a window swap one frame late, and empty texts, captions, cards, windows) are each caught. Every rendered sweep document also asserts that the timeline.json written to its folder equals the table the judges used and agrees with its edit. Not derivable from the edit alone and so checked only by what must hold: how words are grouped and how a text wraps into lines.

### Caption words and letters (review finding 3): closed, with one stated limit

`words.ts`: on the first, middle and last frame of every word's active span (where the group is at opacity 0.25 or more), the caption rect is read as letters and mark. Pill mode is unmixed against the picture under the caption (every caption colour has red 255, every picture red 0: the pill is the blue beyond what that cover leaves of the picture). Lines are bands of letter rows (a band under a quarter em is a pill's edge, not a line); each line must show as many runs of letter columns as it has words, each as wide (from the metrics table) and centred where the layout puts it (centred lines, 0.14 em padding per side, one space); the mark must lie on the active word's place (80 % of the group's mark or more, and enough of it) and every other word must carry under 10 % of it; in colour mode the active word must not show the fill. Documents 07, 08 and 09: 30 frames each judged, 0 wrong (apart from Finding 1). Groups of one word ("GO") are judged too. Controls: LOOK made active one frame early (frame 24) is flagged on frame 24; the lines of group 1 swapped ("MY THERE") are flagged by their letters. Limit: two words of about the same width in the same place cannot be told apart without OCR. Words whose whole span lies where the group is under opacity 0.25 are not judged (none in these documents).
